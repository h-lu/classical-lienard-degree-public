# Matching physical entrances at one exterior maximum

**Input and output.** Fix an inner polynomial \(P(0)=0\) of even degree \(m\ge6\) with positive leading coefficient \(c\), top odd term \(b x^j\), \(j=m-g\), \(g=1\) or \(3\). Fix \(q\) of its hyperbolic cycles in a compact disk and a finite decreasing list of entrance ordinates. The disk can be chosen independently of the outer length \(R\). On the two reflected sides, the actual incoming rays at \(y_i,y_i-\Delta\) must hit the lower section transversely, and their terminal differences must have \(r\) strict sign changes ending in sign \(\sigma\). Proposition 3.3 produces one field with all the compact cycles and \(r+1+\mathbf1_{\{\sigma\ne\operatorname{sgn}(b(2-g))\}}\) further hyperbolic cycles. The exact degree is \(\max(m+2,j+4)\), hence \(m+3\) at gap one and \(m+2\) at gap three. The two exterior calibration parameters serve the whole list. Its length, proximity to tangencies and smallest strict sign margin can affect the required scale.

**Smooth actions and a generic ratio.** In the section maps of Dumortier–Roussarie (2007, §2, Remark 5.2) and De Maesschalck–Dumortier–Roussarie (2023, §2.3, eq. (1)), the exponential action retains dependence on the entrance. For a positive gap \(q_\epsilon\) to the chosen offset, suppose \(A_\epsilon=\epsilon\log q_\epsilon\to A_0\) in \(C^1\) on a fixed neighborhood of \(u_*\). At scaled entrances \(u_*+\delta y\), \(\delta\downarrow0\), the normalized log ratio is exactly
\[
\frac{\epsilon}{\delta}\log\frac{q_\epsilon(u_*+\delta y)}{q_\epsilon(u_*+\delta y_0)}
=\int_{y_0}^y A_\epsilon'(u_*+\delta v)\,dv.
\]
Uniform convergence of the derivatives and continuity at \(u_*\) give \(A_0'(u_*)(y-y_0)+o(1)\) uniformly for \(y,y_0\) in each bounded interval. The 2023 offset is supplied by a reference center manifold. For the polynomial transfer the two arcs of a finite orbit fix the physical reference and the construction must select their common height. [2007, §2](https://doi.org/10.3934/dcds.2007.17.787) · [2023, §2.3](https://doi.org/10.1134/S1560354723040159)

**First variation of the absolute action.** De Maesschalck and Huzak differentiate the endpoint of a fast connection at equal height. For their oriented near-center slow divergence integral \(I=2\delta I_1+O(\delta^2)\), the integrand of \(I_1\) is \(f_e'F_o-f_e F_o'\). Our positive one-sided action has the opposite coefficient \(W=-I_1\) with \(f_e=A,\ F_o=B\). The falling branch uses \(W(v)-W(d)\), the increment from its maximum to the regular entrance. Proposition 3.3 retains the actual even cap, including its common displacement when the odd gap is three. The uniform absolute remainder \(O(\tau^{g+1})\) suffices to match one entrance pair. Resolving the other entrances on the finer scale \(\tau^m\) uses the terminal-map estimate below. Section 3.1 of the earlier paper likewise separates spatial scales to preserve old simple zeros and add two more for two degrees. [De Maesschalck and Huzak, Proposition 1 and §3.1](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10884-014-9358-1)

**The dynamical estimate.** Backwards near the exterior maximum all launches on side \(s\) enter one falling branch. The reference and all launches at an intermediate section span an interval \(B\) of width at most \(C e^{-A/\epsilon}\). Write \(D\) for an integral majorizing all subsequent positive spatial derivatives of the fast field. For \(\epsilon U'=f(t,U)\), variation bounds equal-time separation by \(w=|B|e^{D/\epsilon}\). A transverse collar \(f\le-\nu\) then bounds the difference between two first crossing times of \(U=u_c\) by \(\epsilon w/\nu\). In the log derivative of this crossing map, the common-time integral of \(f_U\) differs by at most \(Cw\) in \(\epsilon\log\) units, and the unmatched time interval costs \(C\epsilon w\). In the last strip \(dt/dU=\epsilon/f\), bounded time derivatives through second order give \(\operatorname{Lip}(\log L')=O(\epsilon)\). This proves \(\operatorname{osc}_B(\epsilon\log K')\le Cw\) for the common terminal map \(K\). The final strip only needs continuity in \(U\). Taking two secants from the same reference now transfers the *ratio* of two intermediate gaps with error at most \(Cw\) in scaled log units. See Lemma 3.4 for the uniform regularity and elapsed-time assumptions.

**Resolution and use.** In Proposition 3.3 the intermediate gaps satisfy a regular-branch action integral with error \(O(\epsilon|\log\epsilon|)\). The whole interval contracts up to the actual maximum’s \(O(\tau^4)\) band, so \(D=O(\tau^4)\) against a fixed \(A>0\). After a common terminal passage, for \(\epsilon=\tau^{2m-2}\) and bounded physical ordinates,
\[
 \frac{\epsilon}{\tau^m}\log\frac{g_s(\tau^m y)}{g_s(\tau^m y_0)}
 =2c\left(\frac{m+2}{m}\right)^{(m-2)/2}(y-y_0)+o(1).
\]
The two errors divided by \(\tau^m\) are \(O(\tau^{m-2}|\log\tau|)\) and \(O(e^{-c_4/\epsilon}/\tau^m)\). The regular-branch slope converges uniformly. The coarser absolute \(O(\tau^{g+1})\) expansion solves the matching equations but cannot distinguish these ordinates. The physical reference starts at \((0,-R^m)\). The two matches impose \(H^*_+=H^*_-\) and \(g_+(\tau^m\Delta)=g_-(0)\), with \(\tau=R^{-1}\). Strict monotonicity then traps the opposite ordinate between \(y-\Delta\pm\rho\) for each fixed \(\rho>0\). Consequently, the selected minus ordinate \(y_{-,i}=y_i-\Delta+o(1)\) gives the exact equality \(R^m h_-(\tau^m y_{-,i})=R^m h_+(\tau^m y_i)\). Each adjusted pair has its own upper crossing, and the whole list uses the same reference height \(R^m H_*\). The matching determinant uses a nonzero odd action moment and a transverse height adjustment. The constructed coefficients converge to the inner field on compact sets.

**Continuing the paired arcs into the core.** The log ratio determines a common upper height. Corollary 2.2a controls what happens after the two arcs return to the large spatial section. On each reflected side the tail has a uniform lower bound \(c_0x^m\). The inward ordinate drift past \(X\) is \(O(X^{2-m})\), and inward propagation of section errors is nonexpanding. Compact flow dependence gives a continuous first-hit map near each selected transverse entrance. Thus the minus launch may move by \(o(1)\) from \(y_i-\Delta\) and the strict signs of the physical differences survive. At an entrance that approaches the equilibrium without crossing, the transverse-hit hypothesis would fail.

**A check with slope three.** Take the positive side, \(P=x^6+x^5+3x\), \(P_\nu=P+x^8/\nu\), \(Z_\nu=\nu\ge1\) and \(y\in[-2,-1]\). The inward ordinate stays below \(-1\) or equals it at launch. Both fields have \(\dot x\le-1\), so each arc from a finite section reaches the lower axis transversely. On the limiting tail the drift beyond \(\nu\) is bounded by \(\int_\nu^\infty x^{-5}\,dx=1/(4\nu^4)\). On \([0,\nu]\) the two spatial right-hand sides at the same ordinate differ by at most \(x^9/[\nu(1+x^6)^2]\). Inward nonexpansion carries the integral of this error to the axis with factor at most one. Integrating \(x^9\) below one and \(x^{-3}\) above one gives
\[
\sup_{y\in[-2,-1]} |B_{+,P_\nu}(\nu,y)-I_{+,P}(y)|
\le \frac{3}{5\nu}+\frac{1}{4\nu^4}.
\]
This is an analytic test of the local corollary with \(P'(0)=3\). It requires only the selected half-orbits.

**Failure test.** The paper's scalar contraction, logistic repulsion and oblique terminal section give \(g_y\propto e^{D/\epsilon}d_y/[1+(e^{D/\epsilon}-1)d_y]\), \(d_y=q e^{(-A+\delta y)/\epsilon}\). For fixed \(A,D>0\), \(\delta\to0\), \(\epsilon=o(\delta)\), the normalized log ratio tends to \(y,\min(y,0),0\) when \(D<A,D=A,D>A\), respectively. At equality the smaller reference-width interval has distortion \(2\epsilon\log(1+q(1-e^{-A/\epsilon}))=O(\epsilon)\), still negligible on the \(\delta\) scale. A wider two-sided entrance window loses positive-ordinate information. This demonstrates failure of a universal exponential bound with zero contraction margin in a distinct scalar passage. The example entails no obstruction in the specific polynomial construction, where the margin is positive.
